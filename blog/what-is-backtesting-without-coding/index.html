What Is Backtesting?

Backtesting is testing a trading strategy on past market data, whether you trade stocks, crypto or both. Think of it as a flight simulator for a trading idea. Instead of discovering in the live market that "buy when the price breaks the 20-day high" loses money in sideways markets, you find out in a few seconds, on several years of data, without risking anything.

Every backtest has the same four parts:

  1. Market and data: the symbol (for example AAPL, NVDA or BTCUSDT), the candle size (1 minute to 1 week) and the test period.
  2. Entry rules: the exact conditions to open a trade, for example "close is above the 200-day EMA and RSI(14) is below 30".
  3. Exit rules: when to close the trade: an opposite signal, a stop loss, a take profit or a trailing stop.
  4. Costs and sizing: broker commissions or exchange fees, slippage, position size and, for crypto futures, funding payments.

Why Backtest a Trading Strategy?

  • Kill bad ideas cheaply. Most trading ideas do not beat simply holding the asset. A backtest tells you in seconds, not after months of losses.
  • Know the pain in advance. A strategy that made 300% may have dropped 55% along the way. Knowing the worst drawdown tells you whether you could actually stick with it.
  • Make rules objective. Writing exact rules removes "I'll know it when I see it" trading.
  • Compare and improve. Test two versions of an idea side by side, or tune a setting such as a moving-average length.

Backtesting Stocks vs Crypto: What's Different?

The method is identical: rules, data, costs, report. The markets are not, and a good backtest accounts for the differences.

StocksCrypto
Trading hoursExchange hours only; prices can gap between sessions24 hours a day, 7 days a week
Candles per yearAbout 250 daily candles365 daily candles
Costs to includeBroker commission, spread and slippageMaker/taker fees and slippage, plus funding on futures
Price historyOften decades for large companiesOnly since the coin was listed
Typical volatilityLower; trends can run for yearsHigher; 50%+ drawdowns are common
Buy-and-hold benchmarkHolding the stock (strong for winners like NVIDIA)Holding the coin (strong in bull markets)
Survivorship biasDelisted and bankrupt companies are easy to forgetDead coins are easy to forget

In practice: stock traders should use enough years of data to collect trades, since a daily stock strategy trades less often, and should compare honestly against holding the stock. Crypto traders should expect deep drawdowns and model fees carefully, because crypto strategies often trade more. Testing one set of rules on both, as below, quickly shows which market an idea actually suits.

Step 6: Check It Isn't Luck, Then Validate

One good backtest on one symbol proves little. Before trusting a strategy:

  1. Test on many markets. "Test on many" runs the same rules on up to 10 stocks or coins side by side. A real edge usually works on several of them.
  2. Compare with buy and hold honestly. The same Turtle rules that beat holding Bitcoin made +481% on NVIDIA stock, but lost badly to simply holding NVIDIA (+2,954%). BitLogic Backtester says so plainly. A strategy that suits crypto may not suit stocks, and the reverse.
  3. Optimize with walk-forward testing. The optimizer searches thousands of settings, then checks the best ones on data they never saw, so you don't just curve-fit the past. See walk-forward testing explained.
  4. Paper trade on live prices. Run the strategy in real time with virtual money before using real money. See paper trading vs backtesting.

What to Look For in a Backtesting Tool

  • Realistic costs: fees, slippage and, for crypto futures, funding. Without them, results are inflated.
  • No look-ahead bias: signals must use only candles that had closed at the time.
  • A buy-and-hold benchmark: beating zero is easy in a bull market; beating holding is the real test.
  • Sample-size and luck checks: a warning when there are too few trades, plus Monte Carlo analysis.
  • Out-of-sample validation: walk-forward optimization to avoid curve-fitting.
  • Multi-market testing: the same rules on many symbols at once.
  • Stocks and crypto in one place: years of built-in history for both, so you don't have to find and clean data yourself.

Common Backtesting Mistakes to Avoid

  • Overfitting: tuning settings until the past looks perfect. Use walk-forward testing. See why a backtest wins but loses live.
  • Ignoring costs: strategies that trade often are hit hardest by fees and slippage.
  • Too few trades: 10 trades say almost nothing. Aim for 100 or more, or test on several markets. See how much data and how many trades you need.
  • Testing one bull market only: include a bear market and a sideways period.
  • Survivorship bias: testing only stocks and coins that still exist and did well, and forgetting delisted companies and dead coins.
  • Ignoring market hours: intraday stock strategies only trade during the session, and overnight gaps can jump past a stop loss.
  • Not comparing with buy and hold: a strategy can make money and still be worse than holding.

FAQ

What is backtesting in simple words?

Backtesting is checking how a set of trading rules would have performed on past prices, before you use them with real money. You apply exact entry and exit rules to historical candles and measure the return, drawdown, win rate, profit factor and number of trades, after fees.

Can I backtest a trading strategy without coding?

Yes. A no-code backtester such as BitLogic Backtester lets you build rules from indicator menus with AND and OR logic and run them on years of stock and crypto data. No Python, Pine Script or MQL is needed.

How do I backtest a stock trading strategy?

Pick the stock (for example AAPL, NVDA, Reliance or Toyota), choose daily candles and a test period of 5 years or more, build the entry and exit rules from indicators, set your broker's commission and slippage, run the test and compare the result with simply buying and holding the stock. Then test the same rules on several other stocks.

Is backtesting stocks different from backtesting crypto?

The method is the same, but the markets differ. Stocks trade only during exchange hours, so there are fewer candles per year and prices can gap overnight; crypto trades 24/7. Costs differ too: brokers charge commissions and spreads, crypto exchanges charge maker and taker fees, and crypto futures add funding payments. Stocks also often have much longer price histories than coins.

Can I backtest crypto and stocks in the same app?

How many trades does a backtest need?

Fewer than 20 trades is too few to tell skill from luck. Aim for 100 or more, or test the same rules on several markets to collect more trades.

Does a profitable backtest guarantee future profit?

No. A backtest only shows how rules behaved in the past. Overfitting, too few trades, ignored costs and changing markets can all make a good backtest fail live. Validate with out-of-sample tests and paper trading first.

How long does a backtest take?

Seconds. Years of daily data run almost instantly. Downloading minute-level data for long periods takes longer the first time.
