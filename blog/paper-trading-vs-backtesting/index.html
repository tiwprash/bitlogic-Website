What Is Paper Trading?

Short answer: paper trading is simulated trading on live market prices with virtual money. Your strategy watches the real market, decides when it would buy and sell, and records the result, without placing real orders or risking real money. It is also called forward testing or demo trading.

The name comes from traders who once wrote their imaginary trades on paper. Today a paper trading app does it automatically: it follows your rules as each candle closes, simulates the fills, and keeps score, including fees.

Paper Trading vs Backtesting: What's the Difference?

Short answer: backtesting looks backwards and paper trading looks forwards. A backtest replays years of historical prices in seconds to show how a strategy would have done. Paper trading runs the strategy in real time on new prices to show how it does now. Backtesting is for filtering ideas; paper trading is for confirming the few that survive.

Should You Backtest or Paper Trade First?

Backtest first. A backtest tells you in minutes whether an idea worked through bull, bear and sideways markets, on stocks over decades of daily data or on crypto since a coin was listed. Paper trading a raw idea would take months to learn the same thing. Use the backtest to throw out the 9 ideas that don't work, then paper trade the 1 that does.

Do You Need Both?

Yes. Each one catches problems the other misses:

  • Only a backtest can show how a strategy behaved in the 2022 bear market, the March 2020 crash or a long sideways year. Paper trading only sees the weeks you run it.
  • Only paper trading tests the strategy on data that did not exist when you designed it, with real-time fills and the slippage you would actually get.

A strategy that passes both, ideally with walk-forward testing in between, has earned a small live trial.

Should I Paper Trade Before Using Real Money?

Short answer: yes. A backtest can be fooled by overfitting, look-ahead bias or missing costs. Paper trading runs your exact rules on prices that did not exist when you designed them, with realistic fills and fees, at zero risk. If a strategy fails on paper, it would almost certainly have failed with real money.

What Paper Trading Reveals That a Backtest Can Hide

  • Overfitting: settings tuned to the past stop working on new data.
  • Look-ahead mistakes: signals that seemed to fire at the perfect price arrive later in real time.
  • Slippage: the gap between the signal price and the price you'd really get, especially on small coins and small-cap stocks.
  • Fees eating the edge: frequent-trading strategies can lose most of their profit to fees.
  • Funding costs on crypto perpetual futures.
  • Practical problems: how many signals arrive at once, how long trades last, how often it trades.
  • Whether you can follow it: watching a 10-trade losing streak in real time, even with virtual money, tells you a lot.

These are the same causes behind a backtest that wins but loses live; paper trading is where you catch them cheaply.

What Paper Trading Cannot Show

  • The emotional weight of real money (losses feel roughly twice as heavy).
  • Partial fills and market impact on large orders.
  • Exchange or broker outages, withdrawals and account issues.

So after a successful paper period, start live with a small size, and increase only if live results stay close to paper results.

How Long Should I Paper Trade?

Short answer: until you have enough closed trades to judge: at least 20 to 30 trades and at least two weeks. Ideally the period should include more than one kind of market (a trend and a range). Fast strategies on 5 to 30-minute candles may get there in weeks; daily strategies can need several months, so paper trade them on many symbols at once.

Stocks trade only in market hours, about 250 days a year, so a stock strategy collects trades more slowly than the same strategy on 24/7 crypto. Paper trading many symbols at once multiplies the number of trades, the same way testing on many markets does in a backtest.

Common Paper Trading Mistakes

  • Skipping the backtest and paper trading an untested idea for months.
  • Stopping too early after a few good (or bad) trades.
  • Using unrealistic virtual size: paper trade with the amount you would really use.
  • Changing rules mid-session, then judging the combined result.
  • Ignoring costs: a paper result without fees is as misleading as a backtest without fees.
  • Skipping the comparison with a backtest of the same period.
  • Going live with full size straight after paper trading.

FAQ

What is paper trading?

Paper trading is simulated trading on live market prices with virtual money. Your strategy watches the real market, decides when it would buy and sell, and records the result, without placing real orders or risking real money. It is also called forward testing or demo trading.

What is the difference between paper trading and backtesting?

Backtesting runs a strategy on historical data, so it tests years of trades in seconds. Paper trading runs it on live prices as they happen, so it is slower but tests the strategy on data that did not exist when it was built. Backtesting filters ideas; paper trading confirms the ones that survive.

Should I backtest or paper trade first?

Backtest first. A backtest shows in minutes whether an idea worked over years of bull, bear and sideways markets. Only paper trade the strategies that pass, because paper trading takes weeks or months of real time.

Is paper trading the same as forward testing?

Yes. Forward testing and paper trading both mean running a strategy on new, live data after it was built, without real money. Some traders also use forward testing for live trading with very small size.

Should I paper trade before using real money?

Yes. A backtest can be fooled by overfitting, look-ahead bias or missing costs. Paper trading runs your exact rules on new prices with realistic fills and fees, at zero risk. If a strategy fails on paper, it would almost certainly have failed with real money.

How long should I paper trade?

Until you have at least 20 to 30 closed trades and at least two weeks of results, ideally covering more than one kind of market. Fast strategies may get there in weeks; daily strategies can need months, so paper trade them on many symbols at once.

Is paper trading the same as a demo account?

Similar. A demo account lets you place manual trades with virtual money. Paper trading a strategy runs your rules automatically on live prices and keeps statistics.

The Bottom Line

Backtesting and paper trading are two halves of the same test. Backtest to see how a stock or crypto strategy behaved over years of history, paper trade to see whether it still works on prices it has never seen, and only then trade it live with a small size. Skipping either step is how good-looking strategies lose real money.
